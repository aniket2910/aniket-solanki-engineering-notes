# 📖 Storytelling — A First-Principles Curriculum

This is a self-contained course on storytelling, built the same way as the rest of your notes:
each lesson traces an idea back to where it came from, explains *why* it exists, shows *how* it
works under the hood, and gives you something to practice. You should be able to learn storytelling
**from these files alone** — no need to buy or read the original books.

A note on what these are. Each chapter distills the **key frameworks and techniques** of a famous
storytelling book, explained and re-taught in plain language with fresh examples. They are study
notes *about* the ideas, not copies of the books. If a specific book's craft ever grabs you, the
originals are listed at the bottom of each lesson as optional sources — but everything you need to
understand and use the technique is written on the page.

## Why this order

Storytelling theory has a lineage, and it repeats. Aristotle asked "what makes a plot feel whole?"
around 335 BCE. Joseph Campbell noticed in 1949 that myths worldwide share one skeleton. Robert
McKee turned that into a screenwriting discipline. Blake Snyder turned *that* into a 15-beat
checklist. Then a wave of modern books (Dicks, Cron, the Heaths, Miller) took the same structural
truths and aimed them at real life: personal stories, brands, talks, presentations. Reading them in
order, you watch the same handful of ideas get sharpened again and again — which is exactly how you
learn a field the way its practitioners understand it.

## 🎯 Goal of this track

By the end you should be able to look at *any* story — a movie, a pitch, a stand-up bit, a résumé
bullet, a five-minute anecdote at dinner — and see its machinery: what the character wants, what
stands in the way, where the value flips, and why it lands emotionally. Then build your own on
purpose instead of by accident.

Start at **00_FOUNDATIONS** and go in order. The lessons deliberately build on each other's
vocabulary.

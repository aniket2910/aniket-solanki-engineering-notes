# 02 — Storyworthy: Matthew Dicks

Pixar and Aristotle are about *fiction and craft*. Matthew Dicks' *Storyworthy* (2018) is about
something you need more often: telling **true stories from your own life** — at dinner, in an
interview, on a stage, in a wedding toast — and making them land. Dicks is a record-holding
Moth StorySLAM champion, and his book is the most practical system going for finding, shaping, and
telling real personal stories. This chapter distills it into two lessons.

| ID | Lesson | Key ideas |
|----|--------|-----------|
| 001 | [Finding Your Stories — Homework for Life & the Five-Second Moment](001-homework-for-life-and-the-five-second-moment.md) | Why you think you have no stories (you're wrong); the daily practice that surfaces them; every story is about a single moment of change; stakes over spectacle |
| 002 | [Telling Them Well — Stakes, Elephant, Backpack, Crystal Ball](002-the-craft-stakes-elephant-backpack-crystal-ball.md) | The devices that create suspense and emotion in a true story; suspense = open questions; the "but/therefore" spine; honesty over polish |

## 🎯 Goal of this chapter

Stop believing your life is boring. Build a habit that surfaces a usable story every single day,
learn to find the single moment of change each one is really about, and tell it so a room leans in.

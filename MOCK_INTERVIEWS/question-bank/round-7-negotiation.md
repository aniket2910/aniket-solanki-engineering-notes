# Round 7 — Salary Negotiation: Scripts for Every Pushback

My anchors: last CTC **19.3 LPA** (say it, then immediately reframe) · target **40–42 total comp** · leverage: **immediate joining**, scope evidence, (build) parallel processes. Full context: [../round-1-hr-screener.md](../round-1-hr-screener.md) Q2.

**The master line (memorize):**
"I'd ask that we benchmark the role, not my last payslip — a percentage hike just carries an underpayment forward."

---

## Pushback → response scripts

### "That's a 100%+ hike. We can't justify that."
"I understand the optics of the percentage — but a percentage is only meaningful if the base was market-rate, and mine wasn't; I entered through a non-traditional route and my comp trailed my scope. The question that matters for you is whether the ROLE is worth 40 to your team, and whether I can do the role — which the loop just evaluated. I'm asking to be paid what this role pays, not my old salary times a multiplier."

### "Our band for this level closes at 32."
"I appreciate you being direct about the band. Two thoughts. If the loop's feedback puts me at the top of the band, the usual levers are a joining bonus and equity — I'm flexible on structure if total lands near 40. And if the gap is really about level, I'd rather have that conversation explicitly: my last 18 months included tech-lead scope, and I'm happy to be assessed for the level whose band fits the work."
*(Levers in order: joining bonus → equity/RSU → sign-on + 6-month review commitment → level reassessment. Never let base drop silently in a "structure" shuffle.)*

### "You don't have a competing offer. Why would we pay 40?"
"You'd be paying for the role, not for an auction. But on the practical point: I'm in active processes, and I've chosen to be deliberate rather than collect offers as leverage. What I bring instead of a bidding war is zero notice period — the position starts producing this month instead of in Q4. If a competing number is genuinely what unlocks the band, tell me, and we can revisit timing."

### "You've been unemployed since April — your leverage is limited."
*(The layoff-discount attempt. Stay level; never defensive.)*
"I'd separate two things. My availability is a benefit to you — immediate joining versus a 90-day notice period. My market value is set by what I can do, and that's what seven rounds just measured. Pluralsight's restructuring wasn't performance-related — I was promoted 18 months prior and earned a spot bonus in my final quarter. If the offer reflects a 'currently between roles' discount, I'll respectfully keep interviewing."

### "Share your salary slips first."
"Happy to share all documentation at the offer-finalization stage — that's standard for verification. Before then, my last salary isn't the input to this conversation; the role's market value is. Let's finish the evaluation and talk numbers on that basis."

### "This offer expires in 24/48 hours."
"I appreciate the enthusiasm, and I'm seriously interested. A decision this size deserves a proper look at the complete structure — I'll commit to a firm answer by [date, 3–5 days out]. If the offer can't survive three business days, help me understand what changes in that time."
*(Exploding offers are pressure tactics; a firm, polite date almost always sticks.)*

### They agree verbally to a number
"That sounds great — I'm excited. Please send the revised structure in writing and I'll confirm within [1–2 days] of receiving it."
**Never accept on the call. Never resign anywhere / decline other processes until the signed letter exists.**

## Evaluating the offer (the part nobody scripts)

- **Total ≠ total.** Fixed base pays rent; variable is a promise; ESOPs in an unlisted company are a lottery ticket — weight accordingly: base > joining bonus > RSUs (listed) > variable > ESOPs (unlisted).
- Ask: base / variable % and payout history / equity type, vesting cliff & schedule / joining bonus clawback / appraisal cycle timing (joining in month X can mean missing the first cycle — ask).
- 40 total with 24 base is a different offer than 40 with 34 base. Have a base floor in mind: **~30+ base** for a 40–42 total.

## The three rules

1. **Whoever needs the deal more, loses.** Deliberate ≠ desperate: build 3–4 parallel processes so this stays true.
2. **Engage every objection; never skip past one.** An unanswered objection stands as agreed.
3. **Everything before the signed letter is conversation. Everything after is contract.** Behave accordingly in both directions.
